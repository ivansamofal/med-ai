HbA1c (Glycated Hemoglobin)
HbA1c reflects average blood glucose over the preceding 2-3 months by
measuring the fraction of hemoglobin bound to glucose. Below 5.7% is normal,
5.7-6.4% indicates prediabetes, and 6.5% or above on two separate tests
indicates diabetes. For most adults with diabetes, a treatment target of
below 7.0% is reasonable, though targets are individualized based on age,
comorbidities, and hypoglycemia risk.

eGFR (Estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate)
eGFR estimates how well the kidneys filter blood, calculated from serum
creatinine, age, and sex. A value of 90 or above is normal kidney function,
60-89 suggests mildly reduced function, and sustained values below 60 for
three months or more indicate chronic kidney disease. Below 15 indicates
kidney failure requiring evaluation for renal replacement therapy.

INR (International Normalized Ratio)
INR standardizes prothrombin time results across laboratories and is used to
monitor patients on vitamin K antagonist anticoagulants such as warfarin. A
normal INR without anticoagulation is approximately 1.0. For most
anticoagulation indications (atrial fibrillation, venous thromboembolism),
the therapeutic target range is 2.0-3.0; mechanical heart valves may require
a higher target range of 2.5-3.5. Values above the therapeutic range
increase bleeding risk; values below it increase clotting/stroke risk.

LDL Cholesterol (Low-Density Lipoprotein)
LDL is the primary target of lipid-lowering therapy because elevated levels
directly contribute to atherosclerotic plaque formation. Optimal is below
100 mg/dL; near-optimal is 100-129 mg/dL; borderline high is 130-159 mg/dL;
high is 160-189 mg/dL; and very high is 190 mg/dL or above. Treatment
targets are individualized based on overall cardiovascular risk rather than
a single universal cutoff.

TSH (Thyroid-Stimulating Hormone)
TSH is the standard first-line screening test for thyroid dysfunction
because it responds inversely and sensitively to circulating thyroid hormone
levels. A normal range is approximately 0.4-4.0 mIU/L. Elevated TSH with low
free T4 indicates primary hypothyroidism; suppressed TSH with elevated free
T4 indicates hyperthyroidism. Subclinical cases show an abnormal TSH with
normal free T4.

Ferritin and Iron Deficiency
Ferritin reflects the body's iron stores and is typically the first lab
value to fall in developing iron deficiency, often before hemoglobin drops.
Levels below 30 ng/mL are highly suggestive of iron deficiency even if
hemoglobin is still within the normal range. Ferritin is an acute-phase
reactant, however, and can be falsely elevated during inflammation or
infection, which can mask a coexisting iron deficiency.
